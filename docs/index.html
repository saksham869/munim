The CFO for every merchant’s AI growth agent

AI agents are starting to spend small shops’ money on growth; Paytm’s Pi agent already suggests ad budgets for small businesses. Munim checks every offer against the shop owner’s own rules before a rupee moves, keeps a comparison group so the result can be measured, and stops offers that don’t pay. The owner hears every decision on the Soundbox, in Hinglish.

      How it’s built

      Munim sits between the growth agent and the offer rails. The agent can propose anything; only Munim can authorise spending, and only inside the owner’s rules.

      The growth agent sends a proposal to Munim. Munim refuses with the exact fix, asks the owner on the Soundbox, or issues a signed one-time passport to the offer rails, which send the offer to the treated customers. Their payments feed measurement, which tells Munim to keep or stop the offer and reports to the owner in Hinglish.

      1. The growth agent proposes an offer: who gets it, how much cashback, on what bill. It cannot pay anyone.
      2. Munim decides using numbers it computed from the shop’s payments: ALLOW, GATE (the owner must say yes) or DENY with the exact fix.
      3. The owner taps Haan on the Soundbox screen, or in the app, when an offer needs a yes. One sentence cancels running offers and holds every new one.
      4. Munim reserves the budget and signs a one-time passport; only then do the offer rails send the offer.
      5. Measurement compares customers who got the offer with those held back, using a return rate pooled across shops.
      6. Munim keeps or stops the offer and tells the owner what it earned, in Hinglish.
      • No model in the money pathThe rules are plain arithmetic on whole paise. The same proposal gets the same answer every time.
      • Fails closedIf anything inside the engine breaks, the answer is DENY.
      • The agent can’t bring its own numbersReturn rates and basket sizes come from the shop’s payments, computed and backtested by Munim.
      • Every payout needs a passportSigned, single use, tied to one offer, its exact customers and the current rules; it expires in an hour. Munim checks it before calling the offer rails, and in production the rails must check it too.
      • Budget is reserved firstDeciding and reserving happen as one locked step, so parallel requests can’t overspend or over-message. Unspent money returns after measurement or cancellation.
      • Edits and rewrites are caughtEvery step is hash-chained and sealed with a key only Munim holds. An edit breaks the chain; a full rewrite fails the seals.

      What’s in the repo

      • Python 3.9+ engine using only the standard library: rules, measurement, passports, ledger, budget.
      • FastAPI service for proposals, approvals, the kill switch and the ledger.
      • 180 tests with pytest, including a check that this page’s rules match the Python engine.
      • Optional LLM proposer for any OpenAI-compatible model. Off by default; its output is parsed strictly and still has to pass the rules.

      How it would plug into Paytm

      • Proposals come from Paytm’s own growth agent; Munim needs no change to how it plans.
      • Segments and return rates come from the merchant’s payment history.
      • Approved offers go out through Paytm’s offers and cashback rails behind the same adapter the mock uses.
      • Decisions and results are spoken on the Soundbox the merchant already has.

      What this prototype doesn’t do yet

      • The shop is synthetic. 650 simulated customers with known behaviour, so the demo can show the truth next to Munim’s estimate.
      • The offer rails are a mock. It behaves like a real API, including timeouts that Munim quarantines instead of retrying, but no money moves.
      • Pooled return rates come from 20 simulated shops. In production they would come from the shop’s own recent history where it has enough, and from similar Paytm shops where it doesn’t.
      • No live model runs in the demo. The growth agent is a scripted reference agent; the LLM proposer exists but is tested only with a stub.
      • No merchant has seen it yet. Everything about owners comes from published sources or the simulation.
      • Small campaigns stay uncertain for a while. Munim says "not proven yet" and measures the next run, rather than guessing.
      • Reach is narrower than a shop’s customer list. Cashback can only go to customers who can be identified and contacted, such as people paying with Paytm, and who opted in. The simulation assumes 93% opted in.
      • The owner’s voice rules are typed in the demo script. Speech recognition on the Soundbox is out of scope here.
      • One merchant, one process. The budget lock and the ledger live in memory. The API’s owner and agent tokens are optional; without them it runs open as a local demo.
